# FORMATS.md — Per-channel calibration

One voice, seven dials. Each format sets how much spoken texture survives, how long the
piece runs, and what the ending does. The VOICE.md rules apply everywhere; this file
sets the intensity. Where a bullet below names a stylistic default (CTA register,
profanity ceiling), your VOICE.md Part 4 overrides it.

## Quick table

| Format | Spoken texture | Length discipline | Landed lines | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Book chapter / manuscript** | low | **preserve coverage; sharpen, don't shrink** | max 1 **per chapter** | none |
| Long-form article / newsletter | low | sections earn their length | max 1 | dry, one line, end only |
| LinkedIn post | medium | 120-250 words | 0-1 | usually none |
| Tweet/X | low | one thought | the line IS the tweet | none |
| Reddit reply | medium | comment-length | 0-1 | none |
| Speaker-read VO script | high | 8-16 words/sentence | max 1, spoken | reluctant, dry |
| TTS script | medium | 8-14 words/sentence | max 1 | per show spec |

## Book chapter / long-form manuscript

The skill's native units are articles and posts. A book breaks three of its defaults, so
this dial states the overrides explicitly.

- **Cut depth is minimal. [overrides CRAFT.md §7]** The 50-percent cut and Mode B2's
  "cut hard" are calibrated for magazine features and do **not** apply to manuscripts.
  Run **Mode B1**: preserve coverage, revoice, tighten sentences, never silently delete
  content a reader is owed. Sharpen the language, keep the volume. The reason is physical
  as much as editorial — a book needs a spine thick enough to read on a shelf, and a
  thin one reads as vanity press. Clutter still goes; chapters do not.
- **Every cap is chapter-scoped, not book-scoped.** One landed line per *chapter*. One
  staccato burst per *chapter*. Book-wide application is meaningless and unmanageable.
  The no-callbacks rule also scopes to the chapter: a book legitimately builds on earlier
  chapters, and a deliberate cross-chapter reference is structure, not a motif callback.
- **Chapter endings: tools are allowed, recaps are not. [refines CRAFT.md §8]** The ban on
  summary endings is written for essays, where re-telling the argument insults the reader.
  A book chapter-end has different utility: a reference the reader returns to weeks later.
  The distinction is **repeat vs. operate**. Test any end-of-chapter block with: *could a
  reader use this without having just read the chapter?* Yes means it is a tool
  (checklist, question set, decision table) and it stays. No means it is a recap and it
  goes. Never write "in this chapter we covered."
- **The artifact is penultimate, never final.** The chapter ends on the author's own line,
  not on a list and not on a callout box. Ending on a sidebar is ending on a footnote.
  Place the tool, then land the closing turn.
- **Named frameworks are permitted where a book genuinely needs recurring vocabulary**,
  which is the one place the cross-format "no coined frameworks" rule bends. The bar stays
  high: a name earns its place only if the concept recurs across chapters and the reader
  needs a handle for it. A capitalized name on a single-use idea is branding, and branding
  is the cliché. Prefer a plain description; keep the substance either way.
- **Front and back matter are furniture, not prose.** Title page, copyright, appendices,
  and consolidated tool kits follow the book's own conventions and are exempt from the
  craft passes aimed at argument.

## Long-form article / newsletter

- Structure: sections with plain sentence-case headings, but no listicle spine and no
  "Challenges and Future Outlook" scaffolding. Sequence sections the way a person
  sequences beats when talking, or just use the next heading.
- **Structure decisions come first** (CRAFT.md §1–2): one point, three unities, the lead
  as a chain of tugs, and each section answering the question the last one raised.
- **No summary recap at the end**, including "the short version" bullets. Find the
  nearest exit (CRAFT.md §8).
- Rhythm: paragraph lengths must vary visibly. Never a page of uniform 3-5 sentence
  blocks — that gait is the tell. Let a paragraph run long when the idea is genuinely
  going somewhere, and let one stop short when the point is finished. Variance comes from
  following the material, not from filling a slot: a short paragraph installed to satisfy
  a rule reads as a drumroll, and the short paragraph only lands because it is rare.
- Keep: hedged recalled numbers, exact cited numbers with sources, a genuine parked
  digression if the draft has one (never manufacture one), math walked in front of the
  reader. A verified story with its weird specifics is welcome WHEN the piece earns one;
  most pieces don't need one, and a fresh idea argued well beats a bolted-on flashback
  every time.
- Drop: spoken filler ("you know," "right?"); self-correction texture survives only as
  one parenthetical second thought.
- Ending: stop one beat early, then the dry CTA if wanted: one sentence, no hype, nothing
  that promises life-changing anything.

## LinkedIn post (medium-length)

- 120-250 words, first line is the hook and must survive the "...see more" fold.
- One point, one receipt (a number, a mechanism, a source, or — occasionally — a
  moment from a verified story), one blunt verdict. No hashtag piles (0-3, if any),
  no emoji, no "Agree?" engagement bait.
- Line breaks between thoughts are fine; the one-sentence paragraph is native here.
  But no manufactured staccato drama runs; short lines are verdicts, not drumrolls.
- Decide your clean register in VOICE.md Part 4 and hold it. Heat stays aimed at
  arguments, never at the person who posted.

## Tweet / X (short)

- One thought, said once, no thread-bait. Two short declaratives where the second flips
  ("Most founders don't fail. They stall."), the X-not-Y contrast, or a deflating
  concrete image all work; pick what the thought calls for.
- No hashtags, no "🧵", no engagement questions. An honest hedge is a fine tweet ending
  when the claim is empirical.

## Reddit reply

Discourse moves for the channel, not vocabulary to sample.

- **Open by positioning, never by greeting or restating the question.** Four moves that
  work: agree-then-extend, scope-relocation ("I think the wording of your question is
  loaded; a more useful way to ask it would be..."), flat correction, validation-first
  for advice-seekers.
- **The middle carries the receipt** — a number, mechanism, source, or lived episode.
  Close on a verdict, a converted lesson, or an honest shrug.
- Register can run hotter than LinkedIn per your VOICE.md Part 4 ceiling, but never at
  the person asking; the warmth rule holds.
- No canned openers ("Good question"), never force-fit a link or a plug the thread
  didn't ask for.

## Speaker-read VO script (the author reads it aloud)

- Write for the mouth: 8-16 words per sentence, subject-verb up front, one idea per
  sentence. If a sentence needs a breath mid-clause, split it.
- Keep the spoken texture that survives naturally: sentence-initial And/So, a
  self-correction if it's real. **Write below the speaker's natural density and let them
  add the rest** — they are reading this aloud and supply their own texture at the mic.
  Do not seed tics into a script to make it sound like them; a bare script they animate
  beats a decorated one they have to fight.
- The legitimate reason spoken texture is higher here is craft, not mimicry: a listener
  cannot reread (CRAFT.md §9). Contractions, short clauses, and audible connective tissue
  make one pass comprehensible. If a piece of texture does not help the ear, cut it.
- Signpost audibly and repeat the key point once in different words; spoken structure
  must be heard, not seen.
- Emphasis is built into word order and sentence breaks, not punctuation. A verdict
  gets its own line so the speaker can hit it. The deliberate non-contraction ("is not")
  marks the one line to slow down on.
- Rhetorical questions work well aloud; answer within five words, blunt.
- **No formulas across episodes (added 2026-09-10).** The sign-on, the sign-off, and a
  show's declared structural beats are format and repeat on purpose. Any other phrase
  that recurs across episodes as the way into content (*worth sitting with*, *here's the
  shape of this one*, *start with what X actually is*, *if this were my company, I'd be
  asking one question*) is a formula the weekly listener learns before the writer does.
  Read the previous six scripts of the show before the gate (LINT §12c).
- Never write applause lines. If a line sounds like it wants a pause for effect, cut it
  or flatten it; the delivery makes the emphasis, the text shouldn't beg for it.

## TTS script (synthetic voice)

- The engine can't improvise emphasis, so the emphasis must be structural: shorter
  sentences (8-14 words), punch word at sentence end, a new sentence where a human
  would use stress or a beat.
- Drop all self-correction and filler texture ("you know," "right?", restarts): TTS
  renders them as errors, not authenticity.
- Keep contractions (most engines handle them well) and sentence-initial And/So; they
  carry conversational flow even in a synthetic voice.
- Avoid words your engine mangles; spell acronyms the way they should be spoken
  ("five ten k" for 510(k)) per the owning show's pronunciation notes.
- If the TTS brand is not the author's byline, use the craft and rhythm rules only and
  skip VOICE.md Parts 2-3 entirely: no personal story bank, no first-person positions.

## Cross-format constants

No coined frameworks. No callbacks to earlier punchlines. One landed line max. Stories
are optional in every format and verified-bank-only when present. Numbers hedged when
recalled, exact when cited. Bluntness at ideas, warmth at people. New ideas ship in the
author's voice without needing their past attached; the voice is the shape, not the
nostalgia. The em-dash and emoji policies live in VOICE.md Part 4 and apply everywhere.

**The one documented exception:** in a book manuscript, the landed-line and callback
limits scope to the chapter rather than the whole work, and a named framework is
permitted when the concept genuinely recurs across chapters and the reader needs a
handle for it. Nothing else on this list bends, in any format.
